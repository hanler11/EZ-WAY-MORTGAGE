HELOC Draw Period: How It Works and What Happens Next

Understand HELOC draw-period access, payment rules, rate changes, and the move to repayment, with practical questions to ask before opening a line.

A HELOC draw period is the period when you may request money from an approved home equity line of credit, up to the amount available under its terms. It can provide access to funds over time, but the line is secured by your home. Before you borrow, understand how withdrawals work, what payments are due, and what happens when the draw period ends.

What can you do during a HELOC draw period?

A home equity line of credit, or HELOC, is revolving credit secured by a home. Unlike a home equity loan that generally provides funds as a lump sum, a HELOC lets you request advances over a stated period, subject to the lender's agreement and account conditions. You do not necessarily have to borrow the full approved limit.

The lender sets the available credit limit and explains how to access funds. Depending on the account, access may be through transfers, checks, a card, or another method. The available amount can change as you borrow and repay, and the agreement may describe circumstances that affect access. Read those terms rather than assuming the full line will always be available.

For example, a homeowner might plan to pay for a project in stages as work progresses. A HELOC could allow the homeowner to request advances over time instead of receiving all funds at once. That flexibility does not make the borrowing cost-free: interest and required payments depend on the balance and the line's terms. Keep a record of each advance and review statements so the balance does not become an afterthought.

How are payments calculated while you can borrow?

Payment rules differ by lender and agreement. Some lines require payments that cover interest during at least part of the draw period, while other terms may require principal payments too. Check the agreement to see exactly what the required minimum includes and how it is calculated.

A minimum payment may not reduce the principal quickly. If a payment covers mostly interest, the amount borrowed can remain outstanding even when payments are made on time. Ask for a written example showing how a payment would be determined at different balances, and whether paying extra toward principal is allowed without a charge under the proposed terms.

Consider how you would handle payments if you borrow in more than one stage. If you take an advance, then later take another, the balance and interest calculation may change. Before opening the line, make a simple budget that includes the required payment and a plan for reducing the amount borrowed. Do not treat the available limit as income or assume a minimum payment is a payoff plan.

What should you know about a variable rate?

Many HELOCs have a variable rate. The contract explains how the rate is determined and when it may change. A rate adjustment can affect the interest charged and may change the payment. Do not rely only on an initial illustration or a payment estimate based on one rate.

Ask which index and margin are used, how often the rate can adjust, and whether the agreement sets a maximum rate. Also ask how the payment is recalculated after a rate change. Then consider whether your household budget could absorb a higher payment while still meeting other expenses. These are questions to resolve before borrowing, not after a change appears on a statement.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau offers consumer financial information at its official website. Use reliable consumer resources alongside the specific disclosure and agreement for the HELOC you are considering; general information does not replace the terms of a particular line.

What changes when the draw period ends?

At the end of the draw period, the line generally enters a repayment period. You may no longer be able to request new advances, and you must repay the balance under the agreement. The payment may rise if repayment now includes principal in addition to interest, or if the rate has changed. The actual schedule depends on the contract.

Before opening the line, ask how long the repayment period lasts, how the payment is calculated, and whether the balance is repaid on a set schedule. Find out whether the agreement allows any other option and what conditions apply. Do not assume that you can renew the line, extend the draw period, or refinance when it ends. Those possibilities depend on the agreement and any future lender decision.

Think through a concrete example using your own budget. If you expect to use the line for planned home work, estimate the balance after each expected advance and ask what repayment could look like once borrowing stops. Then compare that with a lower borrowing amount or a different timing plan. The purpose is not to predict an exact future payment without the lender's terms; it is to see whether the transition deserves more planning before you open the account.

How can you prepare before opening a HELOC?

  • Read the access rules. Confirm when the draw period begins and ends, how advances are requested, and whether account conditions could affect access.
  • Understand the minimum payment. Ask whether it covers principal, interest, or both, and how the amount can change as your balance changes.
  • Review rate adjustments. Identify the index, margin, adjustment timing, and any maximum described in the agreement.
  • Plan for repayment. Find out when new borrowing stops and how the remaining balance is scheduled to be repaid.
  • Compare the purpose and structure. Consider whether staged access fits your plans better than receiving a lump sum through another type of financing.
  • Ask about all terms and costs. Request a clear explanation of costs, closing requirements, and which amounts are determined by the lender or other parties.

What should New Jersey homeowners ask?

Bring questions that connect the contract to your plans. Ask the mortgage professional to explain the line in plain language, and request clarification on anything that is unclear. Useful questions include:

  • How long is the draw period, and what happens on its final day?
  • How is each minimum payment determined, and does it reduce the principal?
  • How can the rate change, and how would an adjustment affect the payment?
  • Can I repay principal early, and are there terms or costs I should understand?
  • What happens if I need funds after the draw period ends?
  • Which conditions could limit access or change the account?
  • How would a smaller advance or a different borrowing schedule change my plan?

A HELOC can offer flexibility, but it also puts your home at risk if you do not meet the repayment terms. Compare the proposed terms with your goals and budget, and make sure you understand both the borrowing period and the repayment period before deciding.
