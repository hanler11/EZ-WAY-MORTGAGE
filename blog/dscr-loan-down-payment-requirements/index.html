What Down-payment and Reserve Questions Should an Investor Ask About a DSCR Loan

Learn how to ask about DSCR loan down payment requirements, reserves, rental income, and cash to close before choosing an investment property loan.

Understanding DSCR loan down payment requirements starts with one important point: there is no single down payment that applies to every investor or property. A lender sets requirements under its program and reviews the property and application. Before you compare options, ask how much cash may be needed for the down payment, reserves, and closing costs—and what could change that estimate.

What does a DSCR loan measure?

DSCR means debt service coverage ratio. It compares qualifying rental income with a property's housing debt payments under the lender's calculation. These programs are designed for investment properties. Some focus more on property income than on personal income documentation, but rules differ. Ask which income and expenses the lender counts and how it calculates the ratio.

Rent alone does not guarantee approval. A lender reviews the full application and property under its own guidelines. What should I ask about the down payment?

Ask for the requirement for the specific property and transaction. Do not rely on a general percentage from an online article or a quote based on different assumptions. Ask whether the required amount is calculated from the purchase price, appraised value, or another basis, and what happens if the appraisal is lower than the price.

  • What minimum applies to this scenario? Ask the lender to explain the program rule and confirm the amount using the same price assumptions you are using.
  • What funds can I use? Ask which account records are needed to document that the money is available and where it came from. Be prepared to explain unusual deposits.
  • Are gift funds permitted? Do not assume they are accepted. Ask who may provide them and what documentation is required.
  • Could property condition affect the requirement? Ask whether the property's type, condition, or intended use changes eligibility or funds needed.
  • When must funds be available? Confirm when deposits, closing funds, and any required reserves need to be documented.

How are reserves different from a down payment?

Reserves are funds a lender may require you to retain after closing. They are distinct from money used for the purchase and closing costs. A lender may express reserves as months of housing payments, but the required amount and calculation depend on the program and application. Ask what payment is used and whether the funds must remain in a particular type of account.

Clarify whether reserves are required for this property alone or also for other financed properties. Ask which assets count, how recent statements must be, whether assets must be liquid, and whether the same funds can satisfy both closing and reserve requirements. Do not count on using one balance twice unless the lender confirms that treatment.

For example, an investor might have enough saved for the expected down payment but not know whether the lender also requires funds to remain available after closing. Request a written estimate that separates down payment, closing costs, and reserves. If the lender's assumptions change, ask for an updated breakdown before making a commitment.

What rental-income details should I clarify?

Ask how the lender will establish qualifying rent. It may review an existing lease, an appraisal-based rent estimate, or other documentation permitted by the program. Ask which source controls if the lease and estimate differ. Also ask whether the calculation accounts for expenses or a vacancy adjustment and how the resulting figure is compared with the property's debt payments.

If the property is not yet rented, ask what evidence the lender accepts and when it must be provided. If there is a tenant, ask whether the lender needs a copy of the lease, payment records, or other documents. The rent you expect to collect may not be the amount used for underwriting, so confirm the lender's assumptions early.

What other cash-to-close items should I ask about?

The down payment is only one part of the money required for a purchase. Request an itemized estimate of closing costs and prepaid items from the appropriate transaction providers. Ask which amounts are determined by the lender, title company, county, or another provider; these are not amounts set by EZ Way. Confirm whether deposits already paid will be credited and how updated figures will be shared.

  1. Write down the down payment and the basis used to calculate it.
  2. List estimated closing costs and prepaid items, identifying who provided each estimate.
  3. Record any post-closing reserve requirement and acceptable proof of funds.
  4. Note deposits already paid and ask how they will be credited.
  5. Gather the requested documentation showing the source and availability of funds.

Consumer guidance from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau encourages borrowers to compare mortgage options and understand loan terms. For an investment-property scenario, use that general comparison mindset while asking each lender to explain its own program requirements; do not assume a standard owner-occupied mortgage guide sets DSCR program rules.

How can investors compare DSCR options?

Compare the full set of requirements, not just one headline down-payment figure. Ask each lender about reserves, rental-income calculations, property eligibility, documentation, and conditions that could change the decision. Keep the assumptions consistent: purchase details, property facts, occupancy, and rent evidence should match across comparisons.

Make a simple side-by-side record. Mark each item as confirmed, estimated, or still awaiting underwriting. Ask who will explain a change in required funds and when an updated estimate is expected. This makes differences easier to spot without treating an early estimate as a final approval.

What should I have ready before asking for guidance?

Prepare the property's address or description, expected purchase details, available rent information, and a general picture of the funds you expect to use. A broker or lender can explain what additional records are appropriate. Ask how to provide sensitive financial documents securely rather than sending them through an unsecured channel.

What should I remember about DSCR down payments?

There is no universal DSCR down payment or reserve rule. Ask about the exact down-payment basis, acceptable funds, reserve calculation, rental-income evidence, and total estimated cash needed to close.
