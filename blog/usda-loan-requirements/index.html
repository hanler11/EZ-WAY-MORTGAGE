USDA Loan Requirements for NJ Buyers

USDA loan requirements cover household income, occupancy, credit, and a rural property address. See what New Jersey buyers should verify.

USDA loan requirements decide whether a New Jersey home can be financed with no down payment through the Section 502 Guaranteed Loan Program. The house has to sit in an eligible rural area. Household income has to stay within the local moderate-income limit. You also have to live there as your primary home and show you can repay the loan.

What USDA loan requirements actually cover

The guaranteed program helps approved lenders finance a modest, decent, safe, and sanitary home for low- and moderate-income households. Qualified buyers can purchase, build, rehabilitate, improve, or relocate a dwelling in an eligible rural area. USDA backs the lender with a 90 percent loan note guarantee, which is why no down payment is possible for people who qualify.

You apply through an approved lender, not by mailing a form to USDA. The agency does not endorse one lender. Shopping more than one approved lender is part of the program design. A brokerage can line up those options and keep the file organized.

Two USDA home loans get mixed up. The guaranteed loan is the one most buyers mean. Household income cannot exceed the moderate limit. The direct loan is a separate product for households at or below a low-income limit, and USDA makes that loan itself. This article stays on the guaranteed purchase rules.

USDA publishes the framework in its Single Family Housing Guaranteed Loan Program page. The binding applicant rules are in 7 CFR 3555.151. Lenders also follow the program handbook. If a flyer and the regulation ever disagree, the regulation and the current handbook win.

Household income limits

Income is a household test, not only a borrower test. USDA says annual income of all adult household members must fall at or below the moderate-income limit. That limit is 115 percent of the area median household income for the county where the home sits. A raise, a bonus, or an adult child who lives with you can change the answer.

Adjusted annual income is what USDA compares with the limit. The program allows deductions for eligible dependents, an elderly household, and certain childcare, medical, and disability expenses. The dollar size of those deductions changes, so use the current worksheet instead of an old blog post.

Repayment income is a different number. It is the stable, dependable income of the people who will sign the note. Co-signers are not allowed on this program. Non-taxed income, such as some retirement or disability income, may be grossed up when the lender calculates repayment. Grossing up does not raise the income USDA uses for the household limit in the same way, so ask which figure the lender is testing.

Look up the limit for the property county on USDA's eligibility site before you write an offer. A limit that works in one New Jersey county can fail in the next. Do not use a statewide average.

Occupancy, citizenship, and repeat buyers

You must agree to occupy the home as your principal residence. Program guidance expects you to move in within 60 days of closing. USDA will not guarantee a loan for an investment property or for temporary, short-term housing. A weekend house does not qualify, even if the address is on the rural map.

The applicant must be a U.S. citizen, a U.S. non-citizen national, or a qualified alien, and must show evidence the lender and USDA will accept. You also need the legal capacity to borrow, or a court-appointed guardian who can obligate you. People suspended or debarred from federal programs are not eligible. The lender checks that status.

The program is not limited to first-time buyers. If you already own a home, every condition in the regulation has to be true:

  1. You will not still be responsible for another USDA direct or guaranteed home loan when the new loan closes.
  2. The home you have now no longer meets your needs.
  3. You will live in the home this loan finances.
  4. You cannot buy that home on your own without the guarantee.
  5. You keep no more than one other single-family dwelling.
  6. You are financially qualified to own more than one home.

If you keep the old house as a rental, net rent usually counts only after a 24-month history, and the lease should run at least 12 months past closing. Plan the move before you assume a second house is allowed.

Credit and repayment ability

USDA does not publish a minimum credit score. Many lenders and the investors who buy the loans still set one. Ask each lender for its own floor instead of treating a number you saw online as a rule.

The lender runs the file through the Guaranteed Underwriting System, called GUS. An Accept finding does not need a credit-score waiver. A Refer or Refer with Caution finding does. On a Refer file, at least one applicant generally needs tradelines with a 12-month history, and the lender submits full documentation.

No usable score is not an automatic no. Non-traditional credit can work. The flyer calls for two trade references when one is a verification of rent or mortgage, and three references when you cannot verify housing that way. On-time rent, utility, and insurance history matter here.

Repayment ability uses two ratios. Standard guidance is a housing ratio of 29 percent of repayment income for principal, interest, taxes, insurance, applicable annual fees, HOA dues, and assessments, and a total-debt ratio of 41 percent. Compensating factors can allow flexibility. Those ratios are program guides, not a promise that a file at those levels will be approved.

One more gate surprises people who already qualify for a conventional loan. You generally must be unable to get conventional financing with no private mortgage insurance on a 30-year fixed term. If a no-mortgage-insurance conventional loan is realistically available, the guaranteed program may not be.

Property location and the home itself

The property has to be in an eligible rural area. USDA's property eligibility map is the check that counts. Enter the specific address. Browsing the map tells you where eligible zones tend to be. It does not approve a house.

New Jersey has eligible areas, often outside the densest towns. Do not assume a suburb is eligible because it feels quiet, and do not assume a small town is eligible because it is small. Population and the official map change. Perth Amboy and other built-up Middlesex County places are the wrong guess. Check the address anyway if you are unsure.

Eligible homes include existing and new single-family houses, condos, planned-unit developments, modular homes, and some manufactured homes. There is no program maximum purchase price, and no acreage cap, as long as the lot is typical for the area and is not principally used to produce income. A working farm operation is a different conversation from a typical house lot.

The dwelling must be modest, decent, safe, and sanitary. Existing homes must meet HUD Handbook 4000.1 minimum property requirements. A private well needs a safe water test. The appraiser, the inspector, or the lender can also require a well or septic inspection. A failed water test or a serious safety repair can delay or stop the guarantee even when your income qualifies.

Financing limits that are not a rate quote

Qualified buyers are not required to make a down payment. The loan is based on appraised value, not only on the contract price. Program guidance allows financing up to the full appraised value plus the upfront guarantee fee. There is no program cap on gift funds. Seller or interested-party contributions are allowed up to 6 percent of the sales price. That 6 percent cap is a USDA rule, not a fee EZ Way charges.

The note is a fixed 30-year term. The rate is negotiated between you and the approved lender. We will not publish a rate, an APR, or a sample payment here, because those figures change and they are not ours to set.

USDA does charge its own fees. For fiscal year 2027, USDA's current fee notice sets an upfront guarantee fee of 1.00 percent and an annual fee of 0.35 percent of the unpaid balance for purchase and refinance transactions. Fiscal year 2027 runs from October 1, 2026, through September 30, 2027. The fee that applies is the one in effect when USDA issues the conditional commitment. Fees can change in a later fiscal year. They are USDA program charges, not an EZ Way price. Check the current USDA notice and ask the lender to confirm the fees that apply to your file.

Closing costs, repairs, and connection charges can sometimes be financed when the appraisal supports the loan amount. Whether they fit is a lender calculation. Ask for that math in writing before you waive an inspection or lock a seller concession.

Documents to gather before you apply

The approved lender's checklist controls the file. These are the items that line up with the rules above, so you can start without waiting:

  1. A photo ID and proof of citizenship or qualified-alien status for each applicant.
  2. Income records for every adult in the household, not only the people on the loan. Think pay stubs, W-2s, tax returns, and self-employment records.
  3. Notes on deductions you may claim, such as eligible dependents, childcare, or disability expenses, with the papers that support them.
  4. Asset statements for gifts, reserves, and any account the lender will source.
  5. The full property address, so the lender can run the USDA map and order the appraisal.
  6. Housing history if your credit file is thin: rent, utilities, or insurance paid on time.

How a New Jersey buyer should use this

Start with the address and the household, in that order. If the map says the house is outside an eligible area, the rest of the file does not matter for this program. If the address is eligible, add every adult's income and compare it with that county's moderate limit. Frequently asked questions

How hard is it to qualify for a USDA loan?

It is strict about location and household income, and more flexible about the down payment. You still need acceptable credit, a primary-residence promise, and a home that meets property standards. A clean map result plus income under the county limit is the usual starting point, not the whole approval.

What disqualifies you from a USDA loan?

Common stops are a house outside the eligible map, household income over the moderate limit, using the home as an investment or second home, a federal debarment, or no way to show repayment ability. Already qualifying for conventional financing without mortgage insurance can also make the guarantee unavailable.

Is a USDA loan only for first-time buyers?

No. USDA's fact sheet says the guaranteed program is not restricted to first-time buyers. Repeat buyers have extra tests if they still own a home, including a needs test and a limit on how many dwellings they keep.

What is the USDA income limit this year?

There is no single national dollar cap to memorize. The moderate-income limit is 115 percent of the area median household income where the home is located, after USDA's adjustments. Check the current figure for that county on USDA's eligibility site before you rely on it.

Which New Jersey towns are eligible?

Eligibility is an address-level answer, not a town list we can freeze in an article. Use USDA's property eligibility map for the specific house. Areas that look rural can still fall outside the map, and the map can change.

Check the address before you shop the rate

USDA loan requirements are a location test, a household-income test, an occupancy promise, and a repayment review. The no-down-payment feature only helps after those pieces fit. Confirm the address and the current income limit on USDA's own site, then compare approved lenders.
