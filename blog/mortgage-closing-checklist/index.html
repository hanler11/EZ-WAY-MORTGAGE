Mortgage Closing Checklist: What to Bring and Review

Use this mortgage closing checklist to organize identification, funds, insurance, disclosures, and final questions before signing for a New Jersey home.

A mortgage closing checklist can help you arrive prepared, understand what you are signing, and catch unanswered questions before your home purchase is finalized. In New Jersey, the exact documents and timing depend on your transaction, lender, title or settlement professionals, and loan program, so confirm your own instructions with the people handling your closing.

Get Pre-Approved

What should you bring to closing?

Your closing agent or lender should give you a list tailored to your transaction. Ask what is required, which items must be original, and whether anything needs to be submitted ahead of time. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's mortgage closing checklist also recommends confirming what to bring, including identification and a way to address last-minute changes.

  • Government-issued identification: Bring current photo ID for each person who will sign. Confirm whether a second form of ID is needed and whether names match the closing documents.
  • Closing instructions and contact details: Keep the appointment information and the names and phone numbers of your lender, title or settlement contact, and real estate professional accessible.
  • Funds, if needed: Confirm the exact amount, approved payment method, and recipient directly with the closing professional using a trusted phone number. Do not rely on changed payment instructions sent only by email. Ask whether funds must be wired or provided another way, and verify details before sending anything.
  • Proof of homeowners insurance: Confirm that the policy meets the lender's requirements and that coverage begins when required for your transaction. Have your insurance contact's information available in case a correction is needed.
  • Any requested supporting documents: Bring or submit documents your lender or closing team specifically requested. Do not assume that previously supplied paperwork is enough if they have asked for an updated item.

What should you review before signing?

Give yourself time to compare the final paperwork with the most recent information you received. If something is unclear or does not match, pause and ask the responsible professional to explain it. You are entitled to understand the documents you sign.

  • Names and property details: Check that the names of the buyers and the property address are correct. Ask how any spelling, ownership, or other discrepancy should be fixed before signing.
  • Loan terms: Review the loan amount, interest rate, loan type, and whether the terms match the final information provided by your lender. Rates and loan terms are set by the lender and depend on the transaction; EZ Way does not quote or set them.
  • Cash needed to close: Review the final amount due and how it is calculated. Ask the closing professional to explain each item you do not recognize. Charges and amounts are determined by the relevant lender, title or settlement provider, county, and other parties, not by EZ Way.
  • Credits and funds already paid: Check that any agreed credits or amounts already paid appear as expected. If a credit is missing or different, bring it up before you sign.
  • Dates and obligations: Confirm the payment start information, any deadlines or conditions, and who to contact after closing. Ask for a plain-language explanation of unfamiliar terms.

How do you prepare for the final walkthrough?

The final walkthrough is a chance to check the home's condition before completing the purchase. It is not a substitute for reviewing loan documents, and the timing and arrangements are usually coordinated with your real estate professionals. Bring the inspection or repair agreement notes you need to verify.

  • Check that agreed repairs or other commitments appear to be complete.
  • Look for new damage or a change in the property's condition since your last visit.
  • Confirm that items included in the sale are present, based on your agreement.
  • Raise concerns promptly with your real estate professional so the appropriate parties can discuss next steps before signing.

What questions should you resolve before closing?

Make a short list and get answers before the appointment whenever possible. That gives the lender or closing team time to investigate rather than leaving you to decide while documents are waiting to be signed.

  1. Is anything still outstanding? Ask whether the lender or settlement team needs a document, confirmation, or other action from you. If so, find out who needs it and by when.
  2. How should funds be delivered? Verify the amount and payment instructions directly with the closing professional. If instructions change, independently call a trusted, previously verified number before acting.
  3. Who can explain each document? Your lender can address loan terms, while the closing or title professional can explain settlement documents and process. Ask for the right contact if a question belongs to someone else.
  4. What happens if a detail is wrong? Ask how to correct an error in a name, amount, or other material item. Do not sign a document you do not understand or believe is inaccurate without first asking for clarification.
  5. What should I expect after signing? Confirm how you will receive copies and whom to contact about follow-up items, servicing information, or the keys and possession arrangements.

How can a mortgage broker help you get ready?

A mortgage broker works with borrowers to compare options from multiple lenders and help explain the loan process. At EZ Way Mortgage Corporation, the process is organized into three steps: Pre-Qualify, Compare Options, and Close Confidently. During the final stage, ask your mortgage professional to clarify loan terms or lender requests; direct settlement-document questions to the closing professional responsible for them.

For New Jersey borrowers, a clear point of contact can make it easier to track what has been requested and which questions remain. Keep a record of the answer, who provided it, and any next step. Do not assume a verbal explanation changes a written loan document; ask the appropriate professional how any correction or clarification will be documented.
