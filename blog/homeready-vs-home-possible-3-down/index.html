HomeReady vs. Home Possible 3% down mortgage: Which Fits?

Compare HomeReady vs. Home Possible 3% down mortgage options, eligibility, and costs to find the right fit for your home purchase and budget.

Two mortgage programs can offer the same minimum down payment and still work differently for your household. HomeReady, backed by Fannie Mae, and Home Possible, backed by Freddie Mac, both provide low-down-payment options for eligible buyers. The differences may matter if you’re counting on gift funds, down payment assistance, or certain types of income to qualify. Before choosing a HomeReady vs. Home Possible 3% down mortgage, consider your full budget, including the monthly payment, mortgage insurance, and cash needed at closing. What Are HomeReady and Home Possible 3% Down Mortgages?

HomeReady and Home Possible are conventional mortgage programs that may let eligible buyers purchase a home with as little as 3% down. They can help buyers who have steady finances but limited savings for a down payment. Each program has its own eligibility rules, and a low down payment alone does not guarantee approval.

HomeReady: Fannie Mae’s Affordable Conventional Loan

HomeReady is a conventional mortgage program backed by Fannie Mae. It may be an option for buyers whose income and other qualifications meet the program’s requirements. Eligible borrowers may put down as little as 3%, though lenders also review credit, debt, income, property details, and the source of the funds. Fannie Mae’s HomeReady program guide outlines its current features and requirements.

If you put down less than 20%, you’ll generally need private mortgage insurance, or PMI. Conventional PMI can usually be canceled once you meet the applicable equity and loan-servicing requirements. Ask your lender how PMI affects your estimated monthly payment and when you may be able to request cancellation.

Home Possible: Freddie Mac’s Affordable Conventional Loan

Home Possible is Freddie Mac’s affordable conventional mortgage program. Like HomeReady, it may allow qualified borrowers to make a down payment as low as 3%. The program is designed to support buyers who meet its income and underwriting requirements, including first-time buyers who may not have a large down payment saved. Freddie Mac’s Home Possible overview explains the program’s eligibility rules and features.

Approval depends on your full financial picture, not just your down payment. Lenders review items such as your income, credit history, monthly debts, and the home you plan to buy. Requirements may also vary by lender, so ask which program best fits your situation and compare the estimated costs.

When Private Mortgage Insurance Applies

Conventional loans generally require private mortgage insurance, or PMI, when your down payment is less than 20% of the home’s value at purchase. With a 3% down payment, you’ll typically pay PMI because you begin with less than 20% equity. Your lender can confirm whether it applies to your specific loan and how much it may cost.

PMI protects the lender, not you. It is separate from homeowners insurance, which covers eligible damage to your property. HomeReady’s PMI requirements provide an example of how mortgage insurance applies when a borrower puts down less than 20%. When You Can Cancel Conventional PMI

For many conventional loans, you can request PMI cancellation when your loan balance reaches 80% of the home’s original value, if you meet your servicer’s requirements. These may include having a good payment history and confirming that there are no other liens on the property. Check your loan documents and contact your servicer to learn the steps and documentation it requires.

If you do not request cancellation, PMI is generally scheduled to end when the balance reaches 78% of the home’s original value, provided your payments are current. The timing can depend on your loan terms and payment history. Making extra payments, refinancing, or asking for cancellation based on a higher property value may involve different rules. The PMI cancellation guidelines explain the equity threshold, but your servicer can tell you how it applies to your mortgage.

How PMI Differs From FHA Mortgage Insurance

Conventional PMI and FHA mortgage insurance both add to a mortgage’s cost, but their payment and cancellation rules differ. With many conventional loans, you can request PMI cancellation after reaching the required equity threshold. FHA loans generally include an upfront mortgage insurance premium, which may be financed into the loan, as well as an annual premium commonly paid in monthly installments. How long FHA mortgage insurance lasts depends on factors such as the original down payment and loan term.

One option is not automatically less expensive for every buyer. Compare the interest rate, upfront charges, monthly insurance, and expected time in the home.
