How to Get a VA Loan Certificate of Eligibility: Guide

Find out how to get a VA loan certificate of eligibility, what documents you’ll need, and what to expect during the application process for your VA home loan.

Planning to buy a home with a VA loan? Before you focus on offers and monthly payments, it helps to understand the Certificate of Eligibility, or COE. This document shows that the VA recognizes your eligibility for its home loan benefit. It doesn’t promise a lender will approve your mortgage, but it helps the lender review your application. If you’re researching how to get a VA loan certificate of eligibility, start by considering how easy it will be to verify your service records. Many borrowers can apply online or ask a lender to request the COE. Others may need to provide paperwork or wait for a manual review. Here’s how to prepare and what to expect.

Key Takeaways

  • A COE confirms your eligibility for a VA home loan benefit: It does not guarantee mortgage approval or determine how much you can borrow.
  • Your service history determines which records you may need: Apply through VA.gov, ask a VA-approved lender for help, or submit VA Form 26-1880 by mail.
  • Share your COE with your lender early: Review entitlement and any funding fee details, then continue preapproval with a separate review of your finances and the property.

What Does a VA Loan COE Confirm?

A Certificate of Eligibility (COE) is an official Department of Veterans Affairs document that confirms you meet the service or other eligibility requirements for a VA-backed home loan. It gives your lender important information about your VA home loan benefit, but it is only one part of the mortgage application.

How a COE supports a VA-backed loan

Your COE gives a lender a way to verify that the VA recognizes your eligibility for its home loan program. You can request one through the VA or ask a lender to help obtain it. Once it’s available, your lender can review the information as part of your application and explain what else you’ll need to provide.

The COE does not replace preapproval or the lender’s review of your finances. You’ll still need to share details such as your income, debts, and credit history. The VA outlines how eligibility fits into the VA home loan process. If you’re buying in New Jersey, ask your mortgage professional how to coordinate your COE with preapproval.

What it shows about entitlement and funding fees

Your COE includes information about your available VA loan entitlement. Entitlement is the amount the VA may guarantee to a lender if you meet the program requirements. It is not your borrowing limit or a promise of a particular loan amount. Your income, credit, debts, the property, and the lender’s requirements also affect how much you may be able to borrow. The VA explains how entitlement and loan limits work.

The COE may also indicate whether you’re exempt from the VA Funding Fee. If the fee applies, it is generally due as part of the loan’s costs, though it may be possible to include it in the loan. Ask your lender to review your COE and explain whether the fee applies to you.

What a COE confirms and what it doesn’t

A COE confirms your eligibility for the VA home loan benefit. It does not guarantee that a lender will approve your mortgage, or that you’ll qualify for a specific loan amount. The lender makes that decision after reviewing your finances, the property, and other program and lender requirements.

Even with a COE, you’ll need to complete the mortgage application and provide the documents your lender requests. Preapproval can help you understand your options before making an offer, but it is not final loan approval. If you’re unsure what comes next, ask your lender to explain each step and how your COE fits into the review.

Does a COE expire or need updating?

A COE generally does not expire, so you usually do not need to request a new one just because time has passed. However, your lender may verify your eligibility again or request an updated COE when you apply for a mortgage. This can be especially important if you’ve used a VA loan before, since your remaining entitlement or eligibility for restoration may affect how you use the benefit again.

Tell your lender about any past VA loans early in the process. They can review your COE and explain whether your entitlement needs to be restored or updated. You can also check VA home loan eligibility through the VA.

Who Qualifies for a VA Loan COE?

A Certificate of Eligibility (COE) shows that your military service, or your status as an eligible surviving spouse, meets the VA’s requirements for a VA-backed home loan. Eligibility depends on factors such as when you served, the length and type of service, and your discharge status. The guidelines below can help you understand where you may fit, but the VA reviews each applicant’s records individually.

Veterans and qualifying service periods

Many Veterans qualify with at least 90 consecutive days of service during wartime or 181 consecutive days during peacetime. The service period that applies depends on when you served, and exceptions may be available in certain circumstances, such as an early discharge due to a service-connected disability.

Your discharge status also matters. An honorable discharge generally meets the VA’s character-of-service requirement, while other discharge types may need individual review. If you’re uncertain whether your service qualifies, review the VA’s eligibility requirements and gather your service records before applying. A VA-approved lender can help you understand what information may be needed to request your COE.

Active-duty service members

You may qualify for a COE while still serving on active duty. In general, active-duty service members need at least 90 consecutive days of service, though the VA considers the details of each service record. You may need a signed Statement of Service from your commanding officer or another authorized official to confirm your active-duty status and dates.

You don’t have to wait until you leave the military to explore home financing. If you’re considering a home purchase in New Jersey, ask a lender about requesting your COE and beginning the preapproval process. The certificate confirms your eligibility for the VA loan benefit, but it does not approve your mortgage. The lender will review your income, credit, debts, and other loan requirements separately.

National Guard and Reserve members

National Guard members and Reservists may qualify through more than one service path. A common route is at least six years of qualifying service. Some may qualify with at least 90 days of qualifying active duty, including at least 30 consecutive days. The rules can depend on the type and timing of your service, so the VA must review your record.

Gather documents that show your service dates and status. Depending on your history, these may include a DD214, NGB Form 22, or a statement of retirement points. If you served in multiple roles or periods, include records for each one. The VA’s COE request guidance explains how to apply and what documents may be required. A lender can also help you check your paperwork before submitting it.

Eligible surviving spouses

Some surviving spouses may qualify for a VA loan COE. This can include an unremarried spouse of a Veteran who died in service or from a service-connected disability. Certain spouses of service members who are missing in action or prisoners of war may also qualify. The VA reviews each case, and eligibility can depend on the circumstances and timing of a remarriage.

You may need to provide documents such as a marriage certificate, the Veteran’s death certificate, or records related to Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC). If you’re unsure which documents apply, review the VA’s eligibility information for surviving spouses or contact the VA for guidance. Getting the records together early can help clarify what the VA needs to review your request.

How discharge status and past VA loans affect eligibility

The VA reviews your discharge status to determine whether your service meets its requirements. If your discharge was other than honorable, or your records contain conflicting details, the VA may need additional information before deciding whether to issue a COE. Submit the service documents you have and ask the VA or your lender how to address gaps or questions in your record.

Using a VA loan in the past does not automatically prevent you from qualifying again. You may have remaining entitlement, or you may be able to restore entitlement after paying off a previous VA loan and meeting the VA’s requirements. Your COE shows eligibility and entitlement information, but it does not guarantee mortgage approval. A lender will separately assess your finances and the loan requirements.

Which Documents Do You Need?

The documents you need for a VA Certificate of Eligibility (COE) depend on your service history and, if you’re applying as a surviving spouse, your circumstances. Some applicants can have their service verified electronically when they apply online or through a lender. If the VA can’t confirm your record that way, you may need to submit additional documents.

Use the checklist below to identify records that may apply to you. Requirements can vary, so check the VA’s COE instructions or ask your lender before submitting paperwork. Gather clear copies and check that names, dates, and service details match across documents. Veterans: DD214 and discharge details

If you’re a veteran, you’ll generally need your DD Form 214, which records your military service and discharge details. Member Copy 4 is often preferred for VA loan purposes because it includes information about the character of your service. If you served during more than one period, gather records covering each relevant period so the VA can review your history.

Make sure your copy is readable and includes your service dates and discharge information. If you can’t locate your DD214, you can request a replacement from the National Archives. A lender may also be able to request your COE electronically, depending on whether your record is available for verification. The VA reviews your record to determine eligibility, so don’t assume a particular discharge detail automatically means you qualify or do not qualify.

Active duty: signed Statement of Service

If you’re currently serving on active duty, you’ll generally need a Statement of Service rather than a DD214. Request it from your command’s personnel office. It must be signed by an authorized official, such as your commander, adjutant, or personnel officer.

The statement should include your name, Social Security number, date of birth, active-duty start date, any lost time, and your command’s name. Check these details before submitting the document, especially your identifying information and service dates. Missing or inconsistent information could lead to a request for clarification. The VA’s COE guidance explains the documentation active-duty applicants need. Your lender can also let you know whether it can verify your service electronically or needs you to provide the signed statement.

National Guard and Reserve: DD214, NGB Form 22, or service-point records

The documents you need as a National Guard or Reserve member depend on your service and whether you were activated. A DD214 may document a period of qualifying federal service. National Guard members who separated may need an NGB Form 22, while Reserve members may be asked for discharge or separation papers.

If you’re still serving and were never activated, you may need a Statement of Service signed by your commanding officer. Service-point records can help clarify your history, but ask the VA or your lender which records are needed for your situation. The VA’s application guidance outlines documentation requirements for different service categories. Gather records for each relevant period, if available, and check that the copies are legible before submitting them.

Surviving spouses: marriage and death records, VA forms, or DIC documents

If you’re applying as a surviving spouse, the VA may ask for the veteran’s discharge documents, proof of your marriage, and the veteran’s death certificate. You may also need to complete a VA form or provide information about Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC). The exact documents depend on your circumstances and how the VA verifies your eligibility.

Before collecting paperwork, review the VA’s COE instructions or ask a lender which documents apply to your case. If you receive DIC, mention it when you contact the VA or lender, since it may affect the verification process. Check that names and dates match across your records. If your name changed after marriage, or the veteran’s documents show a different name, ask whether you should include supporting records.

What to do if records are missing or unclear

A missing DD214 or an incomplete service record doesn’t automatically prevent you from requesting a COE. Tell the VA or your lender which records you can’t find and what information is uncertain. They can explain whether another document may help or whether the VA needs to review your service history manually. Don’t estimate dates or fill in missing details from memory.

If you need a replacement military record, you can request one through the National Archives. Keep copies of your request and any response you receive. If the VA asks for more information, respond promptly and provide the specific documents requested. The VA may need additional records to confirm your service before it can make a decision. Your lender can help you understand the next steps and where to send documents, while the VA’s COE page explains how to submit a request.

How Do You Request a VA Loan COE?

You can request a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) online through VA.gov, ask a VA-approved lender to request it, or apply by mail. The best option depends on whether your service records are easy to verify and whether you’d like help with the process. If you’re planning to buy a home, requesting your COE early can give you time to address any questions about your records before they affect your timeline.

Apply online at VA.gov

If you’re comfortable completing the request yourself, start with the VA’s online COE application. Sign in to VA.gov, enter the requested information, and upload any supporting documents the application asks for. Before submitting, check that the details you provide match your service records.

Some requests may need additional review, especially if the VA needs more information to verify your service or entitlement. If the VA asks for documents, upload clear copies and keep a record of your submission. You can also use VA.gov to check your request status. If you’re unsure which records to provide, contact the VA or ask a lender for help before submitting.

Ask a VA-approved lender to request it

Many VA-approved lenders can request a COE through the VA’s automated system. If your service information is available electronically, this may be a convenient way to get the certificate while you discuss financing. Ask the lender whether they can submit the request and what information or documents they need from you. The lender-assisted request process can be especially helpful if you’re already preparing for preapproval.

A lender can explain how the COE fits into your home loan process, but the VA determines eligibility and issues the certificate. For help understanding your financing options, EZ Way Mortgage Corporation can walk you through the mortgage process in plain English.

Apply by mail using VA Form 26-1880

You can also apply by completing VA Form 26-1880 and mailing it to the regional loan center address listed on the form. Include the service records that apply to your situation, such as a DD214, and follow the form’s instructions for any additional documents.

Before mailing your application, make a copy of the completed form and everything you’re sending. Check the mailing address on the current form, too. A mailed request may take longer because the VA needs to receive and review your paperwork. If you have questions about which documents to include, contact the VA before sending your application.

Choose the best option for your records and support needs

If you have your service information and prefer to handle the request yourself, applying online is a straightforward option. If you’re already speaking with a VA-approved lender, ask whether they can request the COE for you. This can keep the eligibility check connected to your other loan preparation.

Mail may suit you if you need to submit paper records or prefer a physical application, but allow extra time for delivery and review. If your records are missing, your service history is complicated, or you’re not sure what to submit, ask the VA or a lender for guidance. Requesting the COE early gives you more time to resolve questions before moving forward with a home purchase.

How Long Does a COE Request Take?

Online and lender requests when records are available

If the VA can verify your service details electronically, an online COE request may be approved instantly. You can apply through VA.gov and save or print your Certificate of Eligibility once it’s available. A VA-approved lender can also request the document for you, which may be convenient if you’re already discussing preapproval.

The timing depends on whether your records can be confirmed automatically. If the VA needs more information, the request may take longer, even if you applied online or through a lender. Before submitting, review your personal and service details for accuracy. If you’re working with a lender, ask whether they can request your COE and let you know if the VA needs anything else.

Manual reviews to verify service

When the VA can’t confirm your eligibility automatically, staff may need to review your service records. This manual review can add time, especially if documents are incomplete, difficult to locate, or need clarification. The VA doesn’t provide one standard turnaround time for every case, so wait times can vary.

Keep copies of the records and forms you submit, and check your email or mail for requests from the VA. Responding promptly with the specific documents requested can help keep the review moving. If you’re working with a lender, tell them your COE is under review. They can help you understand how the timing may affect your preapproval and what other steps you can take while the VA checks your service history.

Mailed applications and delivery times

A mailed COE request generally takes longer than an online request. Allow about two to four weeks for a mailed application, and more time if the VA needs to review your records or delivery is delayed. To apply by mail, complete VA Form 26-1880 and include the documents needed to verify your service.

Before sending your application, make sure every required section is complete and that copies of your supporting records are easy to read. Keep a copy of the entire submission for your files. If you have a home purchase deadline, ask your VA-approved lender whether they can request the COE electronically. That may be a faster option when your service information is available for electronic verification.

Common causes of delays

Missing documents, incorrect personal details, or differences between your application and service records can slow the process. A name change, incomplete service dates, or unclear discharge information may lead the VA to request additional evidence. Manual verification may also take longer when records are not readily available.

A denial may result from a correctable paperwork or record issue, rather than a final decision that you’re ineligible. AHRN’s guide to VA Certificates of Eligibility explains how documentation and record issues can affect a request. If the VA contacts you, respond promptly and provide the information it asks for. If you’re unsure which documents to send, contact the VA or your lender for guidance before submitting them.

How Do You Check Your COE Request?

After requesting your Certificate of Eligibility (COE), check for status updates and watch for requests to provide more information. The right way to follow up depends on how you applied and whether the VA can verify your service records.

Check online requests at VA.gov

If you applied online, sign in to your account on VA.gov to check your COE request. You can see where the request stands, read letters from the VA, upload supporting documents, and download your COE if it’s approved.

Check your account regularly, especially if you’re preparing to make an offer on a home. A request for additional records may affect your timeline, and submitting the documents promptly can help keep the review moving. If you can’t access your account or don’t understand a status update, contact the VA for help. Keep any notices you receive so you can refer to them when asking about your request.

Ask your lender for an update

If you’re working with a VA-approved lender, ask whether they can check your COE through the VA’s system. When the necessary service information is available electronically, a lender may be able to retrieve the certificate quickly. This can be a convenient option if you’re already discussing preapproval or comparing loan choices.

If the lender can’t confirm your eligibility through the system, ask what documents may help. The VA might need service records, such as a DD214 or a Statement of Service, to complete its review. Your lender can explain what to gather, but the VA makes the eligibility determination. Follow up with the VA about mailed or delayed requests

Mailed applications can take several weeks to process. Online or lender requests may be faster when the VA can verify your service through its records, but timelines vary if a manual review is needed. If you applied by mail and haven’t received an update after a reasonable wait, contact the VA to confirm that your application arrived and ask whether any documents are missing. The VA’s COE request guidance explains the available application methods.

Keep a copy of your completed form and supporting records, along with the date you mailed them. These details can help the VA locate your request and make follow-up conversations more efficient. If the VA gives you a reference number or instructions, save them for future calls.

Respond promptly to requests for more information

The VA may request additional documents if your records aren’t available electronically or don’t clearly confirm your service. Read the notice carefully to see which documents are needed, how to submit them, and whether the VA provided a deadline. Common requests may involve discharge papers, service records, or a signed Statement of Service.

If you don’t have a requested record, contact the office that maintains your military files or ask your lender whether another document may help. Submit clear copies, follow the VA’s instructions, and keep a record of what you sent and when. Prompt replies can prevent avoidable delays, although the VA may still need time to review the materials.

What If the VA Delays, Denies, or Gets Your COE Wrong?

A delay, denial, or error on your Certificate of Eligibility (COE) does not automatically mean you cannot use a VA-backed loan. The VA may need more information to verify your service or prior use of entitlement. Read the notice carefully, identify what needs attention, and ask your lender what documents could help. If you’re buying a home in New Jersey, keeping your lender informed can help you understand how a COE issue may affect your financing timeline.

Fix missing or inconsistent service records

If the VA or your lender cannot verify your service electronically, you may need to provide supporting records. Veterans are often asked for a DD214. Active-duty service members may need a signed Statement of Service, while National Guard and Reserve members may need other service documents. Check that names, dates, and service details match across your paperwork. If a record is missing, contact the appropriate service department to ask how to obtain a copy.

Ask your lender which documents may help resolve the issue before sending them. Keep copies of everything you submit, along with the date and method of submission. Veterans United explains how service records can support a COE request.

Clarify service or discharge details

A COE request may be denied if the VA cannot confirm that your service meets its requirements, if your discharge status needs review, or if records are incomplete. Read the decision or request for information closely. If dates or service details appear incorrect, gather documents that support the accurate information and ask the relevant military records office how to correct the record.

A denial does not always mean you are permanently ineligible. The right next step depends on the reason the VA gives, so address that issue before resubmitting your request. The AHRN guide to COE eligibility describes common reasons an application may be denied.

Correct incomplete forms or documents

Review your application for missing fields, signatures, or supporting documents. If you applied by mail, compare your submission with the VA’s current instructions for requesting a COE. If a lender submitted the request, ask whether the VA received the full package and whether it has identified anything else you need to provide.

When sending corrected paperwork, follow the submission method specified by the VA or your lender. Include a short note identifying what you’re updating, and keep copies for your records. If the VA requests additional information, respond as soon as you can. A prompt, complete response can help prevent avoidable delays in your homebuying timeline.

Ask the VA how to address a denial or correction

If you’re unsure why your request was denied or what information on your COE is wrong, contact the VA before sending another application. Ask which record or detail needs attention, what documents it will accept, and how to submit them. The VA’s COE request guidance also explains how to contact a VA Regional Loan Center for help.

For an error on the COE, describe the incorrect information clearly and provide records that support the correction. Save the VA’s response and any revised COE, then share the updated document with your lender. Your lender can explain how it affects your application and continue reviewing credit, income, and other loan requirements.

What Should You Do After You Get Your COE?

Your Certificate of Eligibility (COE) confirms that you meet the VA’s service requirements to pursue a VA-backed home loan. It’s an important step, but it doesn’t approve your mortgage. Review the information, share the COE with your lender, and keep moving through preapproval so you know what financing may fit your plans.

Review entitlement and funding fee details

Check your COE for information about your VA loan entitlement and whether you may be exempt from the VA Funding Fee. Entitlement is the amount the VA may guarantee to a lender, subject to program rules. It isn’t the same as the amount you can borrow, which depends on your finances and the lender’s review.

The VA Funding Fee is a one-time charge for many VA-backed loans. If your COE indicates an exemption, ask your lender to confirm how that applies to your loan. If any information seems unclear or inaccurate, ask your lender or the VA for help before proceeding. You can also review the VA’s home loan eligibility guidance for more detail about what the COE confirms.

Ask about remaining or restored entitlement after a past VA loan

If you’ve used a VA loan before, ask your lender to review whether you have remaining entitlement or may qualify to have entitlement restored. The answer can depend on what happened to the previous loan and property, such as whether the loan has been paid off or whether you still own the home. Your COE may provide information about your entitlement, but your lender can help explain what it means for a future purchase.

Restoration isn’t automatic in every situation, so share details about your previous VA loan early in the process. This can help you understand your options before making an offer or planning a move. Learn how a COE relates to VA loan entitlement and bring any questions about your prior loan to your lender.

Share your COE with your lender and continue preapproval

Send your COE to your lender once you receive it. If you asked a VA-approved lender to request the document for you, confirm that they have a copy. The COE gives the lender information needed to move forward with your VA loan application. Getting it to them early can help keep the process on track, especially if you’re working toward a purchase deadline or coordinating a move.

Preapproval involves more than your COE. Your lender will ask for financial information and documents, then review your application and explain the next steps. The lender also checks the property and loan against VA program requirements and its own lending standards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a VA loan without a COE?
You’ll need the VA to confirm your eligibility, but you don’t always have to request the certificate yourself. A VA-approved lender may be able to retrieve it for you. The lender can explain what information or service records it needs.

Does having a COE mean my mortgage is approved?
No. A COE confirms your eligibility for the VA home loan benefit. Your lender still reviews your finances, credit, debts, and the property before deciding whether to approve a loan.

How long does it take to receive a COE?
Some requests are approved quickly when the VA can verify your service electronically. If your records need a manual review or you apply by mail, it may take longer. Check your VA.gov account or ask your lender for updates.

What if I can’t find my military service records?
Tell the VA or your lender which records are missing. They can explain what alternatives may be accepted and how to request replacements. For example, veterans can request a DD214 through the National Archives.

Can I use my VA loan benefit again if I’ve had a VA loan before?
Possibly. You may have remaining entitlement or qualify to have it restored, depending on your previous loan and property. Share details about past VA loans with your lender so they can review your COE and explain your options.
